We are pleased to submit "Confinement-loss prediction in photonic crystal fiber
SPR sensors: kernel surrogates versus generative data augmentation" for
consideration in Signal, Image and Video Processing. The manuscript is an
extended version of our paper presented at SIU 2026, prepared following your
invitation to the authors of that conference.

Designing photonic crystal fiber based surface plasmon resonance sensors
requires repeated full-vectorial finite element simulation, and that cost, not
any modelling difficulty, is what limits practical design. The established
remedy is a machine-learned surrogate; because deep surrogates need more data
than simulation can cheaply supply, recent work has turned to generative
adversarial networks to synthesise the missing samples.

We show that this step is unnecessary for models suited to the data regime, and
that it is actively harmful. A Bayesian-optimised support vector regressor
trained on 432 finite element samples alone reaches a mean squared error of
0.121 against 0.314 for the published GAN-augmented deep network. Adding the
same synthetic samples raises that error to 0.197 and causes Gaussian process
regression to fail outright. Rather than report the failure, we identify its
mechanism: generated sample pairs that are near-identical in the input space
yet carry incompatible targets, which drives the covariance matrix towards
singularity. Timing measurements show the generative step to be the most
expensive stage of the pipeline while reducing accuracy.

The work also yields design guidance. Shapley attribution attributes confinement
loss primarily to the outer cladding hole diameters, consistent with their role
in gating evanescent leakage, and a Monte Carlo analysis identifies the lattice
pitch as the dominant source of resonance instability under fabrication
tolerance. We believe the results will interest readers working on
machine-learned surrogates for physical systems more broadly, since the
augmentation failure we characterise is not specific to this sensor.

Relative to the conference version, the article adds per-geometry
cross-validation of every model, the mechanistic account and supporting evidence
for the Gaussian process failure, the manufacturing-tolerance analysis, measured
training and inference costs, and an expanded treatment of related work and the
evaluation protocol. In the course of preparing it we also corrected two results
carried over from the conference paper: the spectral sensitivity, which was
reported with an incorrect unit label, and the tolerance analysis, which had
been evaluated at a geometry outside the sampled design space. Both corrections
are stated explicitly in the manuscript.

The manuscript is prepared in the Springer Nature template with the [iicol]
option and is 10 pages, with supporting material supplied as Online Resource 1.
All figures and tables have been newly prepared for this submission and none is
reproduced from the conference paper. The work is original, is not under
consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved the submission.
